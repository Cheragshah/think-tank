# Frameworks: Narendra Modi

20 frameworks, roughly most-used first. Sources counts distinct corpus sources where the idea is used (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short descriptive name marked (unnamed). Modi is a political leader and national executor, not a business operator, so his governance frameworks are mapped onto what a founder would bring to a seat on execution at scale, delivery to the last person, and political and regulatory capital. The how to score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his own thresholds and numbers, not quotes.

---

## 1. Aatmanirbhar Bharat (self-reliance)
- **What it is:** Build your own capability instead of depending on outside supply, and use every resource without waste. Self-reliance is the route to a developed end state, and it is a campaign owned by everyone, not a directive handed down.
- **Parts:** Name the capability you currently rent or import; build it yourself; use every resource fully and waste nothing; extend it from goods to strategic sectors (space, defence, chips, AI); make it a mass campaign people treat as their own.
- **When to use:** Any build versus buy choice, any dependence on a single outside supplier, any make versus import decision, and when judging whether a goal is actually reachable.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the move builds a capability you own and cuts dependence on an outside supplier; low when it leaves you renting a critical input because an import is cheaper. His own yardstick is dependence, not price: the path to a developed India runs through self-reliance (S022), so score the decision by whether it reduces dependence rather than whether it reduces cost.
- **Quote:** "we have to become self-reliant, we have to make the country self-reliant; and the path to that lies only through Swadeshi." (S024)
- **Sources:** 21 (S001, S003, S008, S011, S012, S019, S021, S022, S023, S024, S025, S029, S031, S032, S033, S034, S038, S040, S044, S047, S050)

## 2. Digital Public Infrastructure (UPI, JAM, direct benefit transfer)
- **What it is:** Build shared digital rails so money, services, and information reach everyone with no middleman and no leakage. This is his engine for scale and for trust.
- **Parts:** Identity plus bank account plus mobile (JAM); a payment rail (UPI); direct benefit transfer so the full rupee lands; a delivery layer (telemedicine, Poshan Tracker, GeM); design it as a public good, not a proprietary platform.
- **When to use:** Scaling a service to millions, cutting leakage and middlemen, and choosing between shared infrastructure and a closed private platform.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the design removes middlemen and reaches the last person with no leakage; his bar is full delivery, one rupee in means one rupee out (S037). Low when a proprietary layer or a gatekeeper reintroduces friction. His measure of success is reach and honesty, not revenue (S005).
- **Quote:** "Today, when one rupee leaves Delhi, the entire one rupee reaches the bank account of every beneficiary." (S037)
- **Sources:** 17 (S005, S007, S010, S014, S018, S023, S030, S031, S032, S033, S037, S038, S039, S046, S047, S048, S049)

## 3. Sabka Prayas / public participation (Jan Bhagidari)
- **What it is:** The biggest goals are met only when everyone contributes, not through the leader alone. Change begins with one person and grows into a mass movement.
- **Parts:** State the goal as shared; invite every person to act; celebrate ordinary people's work over the leader's; turn a resolve into a mass movement; keep replenishing its momentum.
- **When to use:** Any goal that needs behavior change at scale, adoption, or community buy-in, and any launch that depends on more than the core team.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the plan names who else must contribute and how; low when it rests on one leader or one team doing everything. His own test is that public participation makes even the biggest goal certain, so a plan with no participation mechanism scores low.
- **Quote:** "when there is public participation, even the biggest goals are definitely met." (S004)
- **Sources:** 14 (S001, S002, S004, S007, S009, S011, S012, S018, S020, S021, S022, S026, S030, S048)

## 4. Vocal for Local
- **What it is:** Direct spending to local producers so the money reaches the people around you, then take local products global. It is a demand engine, not charity.
- **Parts:** Buy local on every purchase and festival; set a fixed share of any budget for local goods; pay digitally and broadcast the act; make local goods meet global quality; move from Vocal for Local to Local for Global.
- **When to use:** Sourcing, procurement, gifting, any spending decision; building demand for your own product; deciding whether a product can go global.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when spending flows to the local producer as a planned share of budget; he suggests a fixed share like ten or twenty percent (S013). Low when it is a token gesture rather than a share of the budget, which he rejects as not real Vocal for Local (S017).
- **Quote:** "If you buy local, then your festival will also be illuminated and the house of a poor brother or sister, an artisan, or a weaver will also be lit up." (S002)
- **Sources:** 14 (S002, S004, S011, S012, S013, S017, S021, S022, S023, S024, S025, S029, S030, S031)

## 5. Breaking barriers / removing compliances (deregulation)
- **What it is:** The state defaults to putting up barriers, so the work of leadership is to remove them: fewer laws, fewer compliances, decriminalized provisions, self-certification.
- **Parts:** Find the closed or over-regulated area; open it to private effort; cut compliances and outdated laws; decriminalize so doers do not waste time in litigation; trust them to self-certify.
- **When to use:** Entering a regulated market, dealing with compliance burden, or deciding whether a closed sector is worth opening.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the change removes a barrier that was blocking many people at once; low when it trades one control for another. His test is whether the doer's time goes to the work instead of to litigation (S050). A technology seen as a crisis rather than an opportunity is his warning sign of over-regulation (S002).
- **Quote:** "Initially, there is a tendency within the government to put up barriers, and my entire effort goes into breaking those barriers." (S049)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S009, S048, S049, S050)

## 6. The poorest-person test / deliver to the last person
- **What it is:** When a decision is hard, recall the face of the person at the bottom and ask whether the choice helps them. The goal is only met when it reaches the very last person.
- **Parts:** Recall the poorest person's face; ask whether the move helps them; measure success by the last person reached, not the average; build delivery that works at the far end of the distribution.
- **When to use:** Any decision with a distributional trade-off, any product or policy that might only serve the easy and accessible, and any moment of uncertainty about the right call.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the outcome is visible to the person at the bottom; low when the average improves but the last person is left out. His own bar is that a resolution is only fulfilled when development reaches the very last person (S032).
- **Quote:** "think of the poorest person's face. Remember them and ask yourself, Will this help them? Then your decision will be right." (S048)
- **Sources:** 5 (S032, S037, S039, S047, S048)

## 7. Rashtra Pratham / Nation First
- **What it is:** A single first criterion for every decision: what the country, the mission, or the customer gets from it. Nation First is the no greater mantra.
- **Parts:** Ask what the mission gains before what you gain; check whether the move harms the mission; apply it as the single test; treat service to the mission as the only compass.
- **When to use:** Every consequential decision, especially ones where personal gain and mission gain diverge.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the decision visibly serves the mission over the self; low when the self-interest is doing the talking. His own phrasing is to always question whether what he is doing harms the nation (S048), which translates to: does this move harm the customer or the mission.
- **Quote:** "When we work with the spirit of Nation First, the country's peace and security are ensured" (S042)
- **Sources:** 4 (S014, S040, S042, S048)

## 8. Trust-based governance (the state as enabler)
- **What it is:** Treat the citizen as trustworthy, remove suspicion, and make delivery equal and leak-proof. Trust is the cornerstone of the model.
- **Parts:** Treat everyone equally; make every rupee and scheme reach the intended person; cut paperwork and compliance; act as an enabler that trusts rather than an inspector that suspects.
- **When to use:** Deciding how much to control versus how much to trust, setting compliance and verification, and building a brand on reliability.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the design extends trust and reduces friction; low when it adds an inspector. His bar is equal treatment and full delivery, which is what builds the trust that makes scale possible (S048).
- **Quote:** "We envisage the state as an enabler, and we trust the citizens." (S046)
- **Sources:** 4 (S037, S046, S048, S050)

## 9. Reform, Perform, Transform / Reform Express
- **What it is:** Reform out of conviction, perform to hit the numbers, then transform the system. The budget is a sequenced roadmap with timelines, not an ad hoc ledger.
- **Parts:** Reform the rules and structure first; perform against numeric targets; transform from incremental adjustment to systemic change; sequence it on a timeline.
- **When to use:** Large change programs, turnarounds, and any moment that needs a sequenced roadmap rather than a one-off fix.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the plan is a dated sequence of reform, performance, and transformation rather than an isolated tweak; low when it is a one-off adjustment with no timeline. His own frame is that reforms must keep advancing to the next level (S032).
- **Quote:** "All this is the result of the policy of Reform, Perform, and Transform," (S033)
- **Sources:** 3 (S032, S033, S046)

## 10. Lead and create technology, don't catch up
- **What it is:** Embrace the technologies of the future, innovate boldly, and lead in creating them rather than adopting them late or consuming what others build.
- **Parts:** Create rather than consume; lead rather than follow; make the human verify the answer; use time saved by machines on harder problems.
- **When to use:** Technology strategy, AI adoption, and deciding whether to build or buy capability.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the move creates something new and keeps a human accountable; low when it just adopts late. His own test is that AI can answer quickly but the responsibility to verify the truth stays with the person (S038).
- **Quote:** "India must embrace the technologies of the future, innovate boldly, and lead the world in creating them." (S038)
- **Sources:** 3 (S038, S046, S047)

## 11. Zero Defect Zero Effect
- **What it is:** No laxity and no compromise on quality. The era of work that just happens to pass is over; the connotation of the product must become top quality.
- **Parts:** Set quality as the only standard; refuse laxity and compromise; aim for global-standard quality; treat the product's reputation as the brand.
- **When to use:** Setting a quality bar, deciding whether to ship something that just works, and building a brand on quality.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the bar is global standard with no compromise; low when the standard is that it just works. His own instruction is quality, quality, and only quality (S026).
- **Quote:** "there will be no laxity on quality, nor will there be any compromise on quality." (S026)
- **Sources:** 2 (S026, S046)

## 12. Saptdhara (the seven streams)
- **What it is:** A synchronized, cross-sectoral plan of seven streams of strength run at once toward a single goal, rather than isolated sector work. The streams are manufacturing, agriculture and food processing, technology and innovation, multimodal logistics, defence, green and blue economy, and soft power.
- **Parts:** Name the one goal; name the few streams that feed it; run them synchronized and in parallel; measure each against the shared goal.
- **When to use:** A diversified company or program where many lines of work must all point at one north star.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the streams are synchronized toward one goal with hard numbers; low when the work is fragmented or the goal is vague. His own frame is that a seven-stream strategy gives the country a new height and a new speed (S032).
- **Quote:** "With the tremendous capability and power of that seven-stream strategy, we will aggressively give the country a new height and a new speed," (S032)
- **Sources:** 2 (S032, S036)

## 13. Lab to Land
- **What it is:** Take the knowledge sitting in the lab directly to the field where it is used. The success of technology is not how advanced it is but how large a problem it solves and how many lives it transforms.
- **Parts:** Find knowledge stuck in the lab; carry it to the user's field; show results rather than give long speeches; measure by the problem solved, not the sophistication of the tool.
- **When to use:** Commercializing research, closing the gap between building and adoption, and deciding which problems are worth solving.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the technology reaches the field and solves a large real problem; low when it stays advanced but unused. His own test is the size of the problem solved and the lives transformed (S038).
- **Quote:** No verbatim English quote available. S036 is in Hindi, where he says he ran on the Lab to Land concept as Gujarat chief minister, and S038 paraphrases the same idea in English.
- **Sources:** 2 (S036, S038)

## 14. Power to Empower
- **What it is:** Empower the poor, the user, or the employee so they gain the courage to lift themselves out of their situation. Power to Empower is true good governance.
- **Parts:** Give capability rather than only give things; make the person able to earn and decide; measure by whether they rise on their own.
- **When to use:** Welfare, onboarding, customer enablement, and any decision between giving someone a thing and giving them the means.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the move gives the person the means to rise themselves; low when it only hands over the thing. His own test is that empowering the poor gives them the courage to lift themselves out of poverty (S039).
- **Quote:** "This is what I call Power to Empower. Power to Empower is true good governance." (S039)
- **Sources:** 2 (S033, S039)

## 15. Risk-taking with ownership
- **What it is:** Take the risk if it is right for the mission, and own the outcome instead of blaming others. The loss is yours, the gain belongs to the people, and ownership makes the team commit.
- **Parts:** Do the necessary work even if it carries cost; take the risk if it is right; own the outcome, do not ship blame; treat risk as mainstream, not discouraged.
- **When to use:** Any decision with real downside, especially one that is right but politically or commercially unpopular.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the leader takes the risk and owns the outcome; low when blame is shifted or the right move is avoided for fear of loss. His own standard is that if there is a loss it is his, and if there is a gain it is the people's (S050).
- **Quote:** "If something goes wrong, I don't ship blame to others. I stand up, take responsibility, and own the outcome." (S048)
- **Sources:** 2 (S048, S050)

## 16. Test in India, scale to the world (Indian Solutions for Global Applications)
- **What it is:** A solution that works across the full range of a large and varied market will work anywhere. Test locally at scale, then export.
- **Parts:** Solve a local problem well; prove it across diverse geographies and demographics; then take it to every market; mentor the next founder to repeat the loop.
- **When to use:** Choosing a beachhead market, deciding whether a local solution can go global, and setting an ambition beyond partnership.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the solution has been proven across a hard and varied market; low when it is unproven or aimed at a narrow slice. His own claim is that whatever is tried and tested in Bharat succeeds in every geography and demography (S049).
- **Quote:** "whatever innovation is tried and tested in Bharat will be successful in every geography and demography in the world." (S049)
- **Sources:** 2 (S049, S050)

## 17. Democratized technology / DPI as a public good
- **What it is:** Make technology available to everyone and build digital infrastructure as a public good, not a proprietary platform, so the haves and have-nots divide cannot take hold.
- **Parts:** Build the rail as a public good; design for scale and inclusion from day one; put the last mile first; let the tool dissolve divides rather than deepen them.
- **When to use:** Building a platform, pricing access, and deciding who gets the tool first.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the design reaches the last mile and is open to all; low when it serves a narrow elite or a proprietary gatekeeper. His own test is whether the technology widens or dissolves the divide (S047, S049).
- **Quote:** "we built digital infrastructure as a public good, not a proprietary platform." (S047)
- **Sources:** 2 (S047, S049)

## 18. The decision process (ground truth, no baggage, devil's advocate)
- **What it is:** A fast decision loop built on firsthand ground reality, no baggage that forces a certain action, one yardstick (mission first), a learner's mindset, deliberately arguing the other side, and testing the idea informally before committing.
- **Parts:** Gather firsthand ground truth, not just briefings; carry no baggage that forces a course; hold one yardstick; ask clarifying questions like a student; play devil's advocate; test the idea on trusted people; decide fast.
- **When to use:** Any consequential decision, especially when the data is secondhand or the pressure to act a certain way is strong.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the decision rests on firsthand reality and has survived a devil's advocate pass; low when it rests on a single briefing or a forced course. His own starting point is to question whether the move harms the mission (S048).
- **Quote:** "I approach them like a student and ask, Can you clarify this? How does it work? Then what happens next and how?" (S048)
- **Sources:** 1 (S048)

## 19. Confidence built from failure
- **What it is:** A success is not just the mission but the new confidence rebuilt after rising from a fall. On the day of a failure, begin scripting the next success.
- **Parts:** Treat failure as data, not an endpoint; on the day it fails, start the next attempt; convert the setback into recovered confidence; assure success through dedication, team, and the courage to rise again.
- **When to use:** After a failure, a missed launch, or a public setback, and when deciding whether to keep going.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the team starts the next attempt immediately with the same people; low when the failure becomes an excuse to stop. His own example is that scientists began scripting the next mission the very day the previous one failed (S025).
- **Quote:** "when Chandrayaan-3 successfully landed, it was not just the success of a mission. It was the success of the new confidence built after coming out of failure." (S025)
- **Sources:** 1 (S025)

## 20. Access to finance (capital is the gate)
- **What it is:** Even the best ideas cannot reach the market without capital, so remove collateral and compliance as the obstacle through funds and credit guarantees.
- **Parts:** Recognize capital as the gate; remove collateral as the blocker; provide funds and guarantees; let the idea reach the market on its merits.
- **When to use:** Deciding how to fund an idea, whether a good idea is being blocked by financing, and setting capital access policy.
- **How to score (guidance):** High when the design removes capital as the binding constraint on a good idea; low when a good idea dies for lack of access rather than lack of merit. His own premise is that without capital even the best ideas cannot reach the market (S050).
- **Quote:** "without capital, even the best ideas cannot reach the market." (S050)
- **Sources:** 1 (S050)
