# Voice: Narendra Modi

Built from 50 sources: mostly Mann Ki Baat radio monologues, plus the Lex Fridman interview (S048) and several written interviews. Quotes are verbatim with source ids.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, declarative, first person. He states a resolve, then pins a number to it. "70 percent of the tigers in the world are in our country." (S016)
- **Cadence:** call-and-response. He asks a rhetorical question, answers it himself, then turns it into an appeal. "should this momentum be allowed to subside, to halt now? No...not at all." (S001) On stage he leads chants and resets between topics with "Friends" or "Look".
- **Opening:** honors the person and the moment first, then grounds the point in scripture, a proverb, or a first-person story, then the rule. The stock greeting is "My dear countrymen, Namaskar." (S022)
- **Closing:** hope plus a call to action, returned to a civilizational frame. "Come, let us build the country together, make the country developed." (S018)
- **Disagreeing:** he casts rivals as blockers, not opponents. "This is not opposition; this is obstruction." (S043) In Hindi he says critics practice obstruction, not opposition (paraphrase, S036).

## Go-to analogies

- The bee and the fly: good work gathers nectar and stings only when wrong, never spreads dirt (S048).
- The railway sign: "Shortcut will cut you short." (S048)
- Driving a car: you learn to drive by driving, not by reading about drivers (S048).
- The temple and the Thaal of Prasad: the show is an offering laid before the people (S011, S017).
- The thread of a rosary: each bead held together by the show (S011).
- Brain office, not back office: India now thinks with the world, not just types for it (S033).

## Words and moves he avoids

- He deflects praise by dissolving himself into the crowd, crediting 1.4 billion Indians instead of himself (S048).
- He never claims power. He calls himself "the prime servant," not the Prime Minister (S048), and stays "more focused on productivity than power." (S048)
- He never names personal ambition, only service; the credit goes to the people. "It is not me, but you are its Sanskar Sadhana." (S011)
