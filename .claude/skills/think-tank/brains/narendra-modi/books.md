# Books: Narendra Modi

Modi authored three books, but none are in the corpus, so their text is not reproduced. What the brain actually learns from is his public work: the Mann Ki Baat radio addresses (S001 to S031), major speeches (S032 to S043), written interviews (S044 to S047), the long Lex Fridman interview (S048), and the two startup speeches (S049, S050). Those are the source-backed teaching material.

## Books he authored (not in the corpus)

- **Exam Warriors (2018):** A book for students on facing exams, written as a dialogue. Listed factually here; no text is reproduced.
- **Convenient Action (2015):** Essays on governance framed around "convenient action," meaning decisive and timely steps rather than inertia. Listed factually; no text is reproduced.
- **Abode of Love:** A collection of his poems. Listed factually; no text is reproduced.

These three are named in the seat build as his authored works, but since none is in the corpus, the brain's frameworks, principles, and quotes come from the public work above, not from these books.

## Books and works he leans on (endorsed, not authored)

- **A pictorial biography of Sardar Patel (Ministry of Information and Broadcasting).** He urges all young people to read it to know Sardar Patel in an interesting way, and ties it to national unity as a precondition for progress (S002).
- **S. L. Bhyrappa's works (Kannada).** He says Bhyrappa taught the importance of pride in one's roots and culture, and urges youth to read his oeuvre (S024).
- **Bhai Parmanand's Aapbeeti: Kaale Paani Ki Kaaraavaas Kahaani.** He calls it a must-read to truly understand the sacrifices of freedom fighters (S031).
- **Chanakya's Arthashastra.** He cites it for the concept of Matsya Nyaya, that when a system is weak the big fish swallows the small, and that the world needs a system that prevents that (S033).
- **Bhai Jagdish Trivedi's Social Audit of Social Service.** He describes it as a unique balance sheet, a full account of the author's six years of program income with every rupee accounted for (S014).
- **A Sanskrit aphorism he cites on enterprise.** In S050 he quotes an aphorism he translates as "work is accomplished through enterprise, not merely by wishful thinking," and uses it to ground risk and enterprise in tradition.

These endorsed works teach the brain the same ideas his own speeches teach: pride in roots, unity, sacrifice, order over anarchy, and work through enterprise rather than wishful thinking.
