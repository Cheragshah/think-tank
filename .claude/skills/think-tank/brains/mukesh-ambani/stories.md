# Stories: Mukesh Ambani

16 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **Dhirubhai's small beginnings.** Reliance began with Dhirubhai arriving in Mumbai in 1960 with a hundred dollars, one chair, one table, and a telephone, and later a single-room office with two tables and a shared landline. Lesson: start tiny, but build something consequential for India, not just wealth (S005, S007, S025).
2. **Jio as the biggest risk.** He put roughly $25 billion of his own money into Jio, as majority shareholder, while analysts doubted India was ready, and accepted the worst case might be a financial loss. Lesson: you can make a bold bet only if you can survive the worst-case scenario (S024, S025).
3. **The decades of dreams.** Polyester in the 1970s, polymers in the 1980s, fuels in the 1990s, hydrocarbons, then retail and digital, each dream bigger and each forcing reinvention. Lesson: reinvent the business on a fixed cadence of three to five years (S004, S024).
4. **Jio ended digital poverty.** By making data affordable, Jio turned a data dark India into a data rich nation in under a decade. Lesson: infrastructure priced for the mass market can flip a whole country's trajectory (S002, S013).
5. **The sceptics and 100 million customers.** Sceptics said free users would never convert to paid; Jio hit 100 million signups in under 170 days, faster than Facebook, WhatsApp or Skype. Lesson: conviction plus an ambitious target beats analyst consensus (S020, S026).
6. **The five-minute BlackRock pitch.** He convinced Larry Fink in five minutes, in a car ride, that BlackRock should return to India. Lesson: when conviction and trust align, a big deal needs no long courtship (S015).
7. **Unproductive saving.** India imports about $60 billion of gold and $10 to $15 billion of silver for non-industrial saving that does not compound. Lesson: redirect savings into productive, compounding capital instead of inert assets (S015).
8. **Bioenergy from stubble burning.** He turned the crop-stubble pollution problem into India's largest bio-energy business. Lesson: convert a societal problem into a commercial engine (S003).
9. **JIOMEET built in two months.** A young Jio team built the AGM's video platform in two months during COVID, with millions of downloads in days. Lesson: adversity is the chance to build and demonstrate your own product (S019).
10. **JioMart and the kiranas.** Piloted with kirana partners, JioMart reached 250,000 orders a day within weeks. Lesson: partnering with small merchants scales a platform faster than competing with them (S019).
11. **Saving the local cable operators.** DTH was killing local cable operators, so he bought the MSOs and engineered the Jio set-top-box to carry their signals so partners keep their revenue. Lesson: scale works only when it carries your small partners along (S022).
12. **Isha and the slow internet.** His daughter came home from Yale in 2010 and said the internet at home was bad; his son told him he would not get it. That seeded Jio. Lesson: seed decisions to the next generation and learn from them (S016).
13. **The translator analogy.** Legacy networks retrofit data onto voice networks, like speaking through a translator instead of your mother tongue. Lesson: build natively for the new paradigm, do not retrofit (S010).
14. **Aadhaar unlocked onboarding.** National digital identity let Jio onboard millions of customers in a few months. Lesson: national digital infrastructure is a compounding asset for scale (S017).
15. **Jamnagar medical oxygen.** He repurposed the Jamnagar plants into the largest single-location producer of medical-grade oxygen during COVID. Lesson: speed and scale of execution, not just strategy, is the firm's edge (S014).
16. **The media foray that did not work.** His first entry into media failed, and he notes most of his businesses were greenfield while media was brownfield. Lesson: even he has misfires, and he admits them (S016).
